Why is the sky blue, but a sunset red ?
A N C o mmu nity p ic k
The short answer :  sunlight takes a different j ourney through the atmosphere at sunset.
Air molecules scatter shorter wavelengths more strongly than longer ones. In the Rayleigh regime, the scattering intensity
scales roughly as 1 / λ⁴ . Blue light is scattered across the sky far more effectively than red light.
I ∝ 1 / λ⁴
Shorter wavelength →  stronger scattering
When the Sun is low, its light travels through a much longer atmospheric path. Much of the blue light is scattered away before
the direct beam reaches us. What remains is richer in reds and oranges.
The sky isn ’ t blue because air is blue. It ’ s blue because of what air does to light —  and where we happen to be looking.
